  \begin{abstract}
    The spectral gravity sub-programme of the Cosmochrony corpus addresses a single
    central question: why does the effective metric $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ satisfy
    the Einstein equations?
    Starting from the projective spectral entropy functional
    $\SPI[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' A_g$, the sub-programme derives the Einstein tensor
    as the infrared-dominant response of the renormalized metric variation, reformulates
    it as a spectral equilibrium condition, extends it causally to gravitational wave
    propagation, and provides its ultraviolet Born--Infeld completion.
    The logical chain is:
    \[
      \SPI[g]
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \delta_{g}\SPI \ni \cEH G_{\mu\nu}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \text{causal completion}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \text{BI completion}.
    \]
    The sub-programme is constituted by four papers: Gravity~3.0, Thermodynamics,
    Lorentz/CausalPropagation, and BornInfeld.
    The scope is the gravitational sector in the absence of matter; full coupled field equations
    $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}$ with matter in Lorentzian signature remain an open
    deliverable.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    The spectral gravity sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The emergent geometry sub-programme (Note~2) derives the effective Lorentzian metric
    $g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ as a forced consequence of the admissibility structure.
    It does not explain why this metric is dynamical, nor why its dynamics takes the Einstein
    form.
    \textit{Why does the projective spectral entropy functional $\SPI[g]$ produce the Einstein
    equations as its metric variation?}

    \medskip
    More precisely: define $A_g = -\nabla_g^2$ as the minimal admissible Laplace-type
    operator on the effective four-dimensional manifold $M$, and
    \[
      \SPI[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' A_g
    \]
    as the projective spectral entropy functional.
    The question is: what is the geometric content of $\delta\SPI/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, and
    in which regime does it reduce to the Einstein tensor?

    \medskip
    The answer has four stages resolved by four papers.
    Gravity derives the Einstein tensor as the $a_2$ infrared response.
    Thermodynamics reformulates this as a spectral equilibrium condition equivalent to
    the Einstein field equations.
    Lorentz/CausalPropagation completes the construction causally and identifies
    gravitational waves as infrared probes of the pre-geometric scale.
    BornInfeld provides the unique ultraviolet completion of the Einstein--Hilbert sector.

    \begin{remark}[Scope: gravity sector only]
This sub-programme closes the gravitational dynamics in the absence of matter.
The coupled system $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}$ with explicit matter in Lorentzian
signature is an open deliverable listed in Section~\ref{sec:open}.
The gauge dynamics ($a_4$ Yang--Mills response from the vertical variation) belongs to
Note~8 (Gauge--Gravity Stratification Sub-Programme).
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Distinction from Note~2]
Note~2 (Emergent Geometry) closes the \emph{reconstruction} of the metric:
$\Pi_q \to g^{\mu\nu} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}$.
Note~4 (Spectral Gravity) closes the \emph{dynamics} of the metric:
$\SPI[g] \to G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}$.
The two notes address logically distinct questions and should not be conflated.
    \end{remark}

      Imposing a spectral equilibrium principle (extremize $\SPI$ at fixed projected matter
      content) yields the Einstein field equations as a variational Euler--Lagrange condition,
      without any assumption on causal structure
      (Thermodynamics~\cite{Beau2026Thermodynamics}).

      \item \textit{Causal completion.}
      The Lorentzian spectral action is defined via the Schwinger--Keldysh prescription.
      Linearization about Minkowski spacetime gives two transverse-traceless helicity-$\pm 2$
      modes and a dispersion relation $\omega^2 = c^2 k^2 - \gamma\lsp^2 k^4$,

      Under conditions (C1)--(C5) and Hypothesis~[H-ext], the unique admissible tensorial

    \subsection{Infrared Einstein response: Gravity 3.0}
      \label{sec:gravity}

      \textbf{Gravity 3.0}~\cite{Beau2026h} is the foundational paper of the sub-programme.
      It establishes the projective spectral entropy functional $\SPI[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' A_g$
      for a minimal Laplace-type operator $A_g = -\nabla_g^2$ on a four-dimensional Riemannian
      manifold, and derives the renormalized metric variation via heat-kernel methods and
      zeta regularization.

      Three results are established.

      \textit{IR hierarchy} (Theorem of §4.5; structural).
      The renormalized spectral stress tensor decomposes as
      \[
        \frac{\delta\SPI^{\mathrm{ren}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
        = \cEH(\mu)\, G_{\mu\nu}
        + c_\Lambda(\mu)\, g_{\mu\nu}
        + \beta_{\mathrm{loc}}\, B_{\mu\nu}
        + T^{\mathrm{non-loc}}_{\mu\nu}
        + \mathcal{A}_{\mu\nu},
      \]
      where $B_{\mu\nu}$ contains quadratic curvature terms ($a_4$), $T^{\mathrm{non-loc}}_{\mu\nu}$
      are non-local form factors, and $\mathcal{A}_{\mu\nu}$ is the conformal anomaly.
      In the regime $R\lsp^2 \ll 1$, the $a_2$ Einstein term is infrared-dominant.

      \textit{Induced Newton constant} (§5; structural).
      The induced coupling scales as $\GN \sim 16\pi^2 \lsp^2$, up to scheme-dependent factors.
      Newton's constant is not a free parameter: it is set by the same spectral length $\lsp$
      that governs the BI completion.

      \textit{BI uniqueness} (Theorem~1; conditional on [H-ext]).
      Under conditions (C1)--(C5) (spectral covariance, Einstein IR limit, algebraic BI saturation,
      spectral isotropy, coherence extensivity) and Hypothesis [H-ext] (admissible coherence
      extensivity, structural analogue of [H-color] for the gravitational sector), the unique
      admissible tensorial UV completion is the Eddington-inspired BI action.
      Lemmas 2 and 3 (scalar BI reduction and spectral carrier identification) are proved
      unconditionally; only Lemma~1 (coherence extensivity) is conditional on [H-ext].

      \textit{Structural necessity of non-injective projection} (§7.2; structural).
      Any genuine emergence with $\SPI > 0$ requires non-injective projection.
      The gravitational response is therefore structurally tied to the non-injectivity of $\Pi$
      and cannot arise in a purely injective effective description.

    \subsection{Spectral equilibrium and the Einstein equation: Thermodynamics}
      \label{sec:thermo}

      \textbf{Thermodynamics}~\cite{Beau2026Thermodynamics} develops the local thermodynamic
      interpretation of $\SPI[g]$ and derives the Einstein field equations as a spectral
      equilibrium condition.

      Three results are established.

      \textit{Local spectral multiplier field} (§2; structural).
      The diagonal heat kernel $K(x, x; t)$ defines a local spectral energy density
      $u(x; t) = -\partial_t \log K(x, x; t)$, whose Seeley--DeWitt expansion gives the local
      multiplier:
      \[
        \beta(x)^{-1} = \beta_0^{-1} - \tfrac{1}{6}R(x) + O(\nabla^2 R).
      \]
      Here $\beta_0^{-1} = 2/t_*$ is a universal UV contribution; the $-\tfrac{1}{6}R(x)$
      correction is the same $a_2$ coefficient that produces the Einstein tensor in Gravity.

      \textit{Local spectral first law} (§3; structural).
      The compatibility between the geometric and matter sides of the metric variation
      gives $\delta\SPI = \int\beta(x)^{-1}\delta E_\Pi(x)$.
      This is not a postulate but a consequence of the Seeley--DeWitt hierarchy.

      \textit{Einstein equation as spectral equilibrium} (Theorem~4.3; structural).
      The spectral equilibrium principle: admissible geometries extremize $\SPI$ at fixed
      projected matter content $W_\Pi[g, \psi]$.
      The Euler--Lagrange equation is
      \[
        G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)},
      \]
      with the factor $8\pi\GN$ fixed by the product of two universal normalizations.
      No horizon structure, no Rindler wedge, and no Raychaudhuri theorem are required.
      The derivation is Riemannian; its extension to Lorentzian signature is the content
      of the Lorentz/CausalPropagation paper.

      The relation to Jacobson's derivation is established in §6: the present framework is
      structurally parallel but replaces area entropy by $\SPI$, Rindler temperature by
      $\beta(x)^{-1}$, and horizon equilibrium by variational extremum.
      The derivations are logically independent.

    \subsection{Causal completion and gravitational waves: Lorentz/CausalPropagation}
      \label{sec:lorentz}

      \textbf{Lorentz/CausalPropagation}~\cite{Beau2026i} constructs the Lorentzian completion
      of the spectral entropy framework and establishes the causal propagation of gravitational
      degrees of freedom.

      Three results are established.

      \textit{Lorentzian spectral action} (§2; structural).
      The Lorentzian spectral action is defined via the real part of the hyperbolic determinant,
      equivalently through a Schwinger--Keldysh prescription.
      This ensures a real and retarded quadratic kernel without additional assumptions.

      \textit{Graviton content} (§3; structural).
      Linearization about Minkowski spacetime in the regime $R\lsp^2 \ll 1$ and
      $\omega \ll \lsp^{-1}$ propagates exactly two transverse-traceless tensor modes of
      helicity $\pm 2$.
      Scalar and vector sectors remain non-dynamical in the infrared.
      The polarization content coincides with that of general relativity in the accessible
      domain.

      \textit{Dispersion relation and $k^4$ correction} (§3; structural).
      The ratio of Seeley--DeWitt coefficients fixes the universal correction:
      \[
        \omega^2 = c^2 k^2 - \gamma\lsp^2 k^4 + O(k^6),
        \quad \gamma = \frac{1}{180\zeta},
      \]
      with $\gamma = 1/30$ in the natural spectral scheme.
      The additional pole at $k^2 \sim \lsp^{-2}$ lies beyond current observational reach.
      This structure maps onto the parametrizations used in gravitational-wave catalogues
      and yields a bound on the pre-geometric scale $\lsp$.

  \section{Open Deliverables}
    \label{sec:open}

    Three open items define the boundary of the sub-programme.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 1: Analytical proof of [H-ext].}
    This is the sole conditionality of the BI uniqueness result.
    Resolving [H-ext] would promote Gravity Theorem~1 to an unconditional theorem.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 2: Full Lorentzian Einstein equilibrium.}
    The spectral equilibrium derivation of $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}$ is
    currently Riemannian.
    A fully covariant Lorentzian formulation, integrating the Schwinger--Keldysh
    prescription of Lorentz/CausalPropagation with the spectral equilibrium principle of
    Thermodynamics, is an open structural programme.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 3: Coupled field equations with matter in Lorentzian signature.}
    The full system $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\GN T_{\mu\nu}$ with explicit matter fields
    and without the current-free restriction is the primary open deliverable of the
    gravitational sector.
    It requires identifying the admissible projected sector for each matter species,
    a task that awaits the completion of Note~6 (Fermionic Matter Sub-Programme).
    This deliverable separates into a closed downstream part and an open upstream part.
    The sourced vector sector is structurally fixed once a projected momentum density $T^{(\Pi)}_{0i}$ is supplied:
    the linearized Lorentzian constraint produces the gravitomagnetic component $g_{0i}$ in the standard infrared
    Einstein regime, reproducing frame dragging without an additional propagating degree of freedom
    (Lorentz/CausalPropagation~\cite{Beau2026i}).
    The open part is upstream, namely the derivation of $T^{(\Pi)}_{0i}$ from moving projected matter
    configurations, which remains to be completed in the fermionic and coupled-matter sector.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The spectral gravity sub-programme derives gravitational dynamics from the projective
    spectral entropy functional $\SPI[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' A_g$.

    The Einstein tensor arises as the $a_2$ infrared-dominant response of the renormalized
    metric variation (Gravity~3.0), reformulated without causal assumptions as the
    Euler--Lagrange condition of a spectral equilibrium principle (Thermodynamics).
    Two transverse-traceless helicity-$\pm 2$ graviton modes propagate causally in the
    Lorentzian completion, with a universal $k^4$ correction suppressed by $\lsp^2$
    (Lorentz/CausalPropagation).
    The unique ultraviolet completion is the Eddington-inspired Born--Infeld action,
    conditional on [H-ext] (Gravity Theorem~1, BornInfeld).

    The sub-programme does not address Yang--Mills dynamics or the gauge--gravity coupling
    ratio; those belong to Note~8.
    The full coupled Einstein field equations with matter in Lorentzian signature remain
    the primary open deliverable.
